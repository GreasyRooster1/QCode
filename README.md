## Foundational Principles
there are 3 major foundational principles QCode uses to teach effectively:
- **Every student follows at their own pace**
- **Freedom for students to select projects they want**
- **Everything lives in the app**

## At your own pace
Teaching computer science in a traditional education environment causes a few issues with students learning the content. Mainly, students work at different paces, so some are either left behind or stuck waiting for others to catch up. Given this, project based learning often leaves teachers tied up in helping specific students rather than teaching the valuable course material.

**How do we solve this?**

QCode courses are designed around students moving at their own pace, and the entire platform is built to cater to this principle. QCode doesn't use video lessons or long paragraphs to read with code to cut and paste. Every lesson is a series of steps organized into chapters. The steps are intentionally small, making students constantly feel like they are making progress as they complete them. Each step contains a small description, and often an **image** some code to write out. Every piece of code a student is told to write comes from an image, so there is never a chance to copy and paste directly into their project. 

Students also dont just copy down the code when following lessons. Every step has a "type", clearly showing if a step is meant to be informational, code to write, etc. These steps include:
- Info - explains a concept to a sudent
- Code - a snippet of code to write
- Challenge - a partial snipped of code, forcing the student to recall previous knowledge to complete the step
- Check - regular checks to make sure the lesson has been followed correctly

## Choose your own adventure
QCode gives students the authority to select what their next lesson should be, based on their current skill level.

When students are selecting their next lesson, they go to the **Lesson Flowchart**. The flowchart is the structure of every course. Students follow the lessons in order, but there are many branches for students to choose their own path. This gives students the opportunity to pick which lesson they want next. When students were forced to follow a linear lesson plan, they often were disengaged with the coursework. By giving them the choice of their next project, they become more invested in what they are building. Suprisingly, given this freedom, **students often end up completing every lesson on their own accord**, rather than using their freedom to skip parts of the course. 

## The one stop shop
QCode is designed to keep students on the platform for everything they do. The built-in editor allows them to write code directly next to their lesson steps. The editor also runs the code within the same window, so students never need to switch tabs or navigate to a different app.

Students are also encouraged to share their projects to each other and view other student's shared projects. The **Share Board** is where students show off their work to others. The Share Board has let students take pride in their projects knowing others will view and interact with them. Students will often use their skills obtained in the lessons to create their own, self guided, projects with the goal of sharing them to others.

## The JavaScript Course
The JavaScript course features 16 lessons with about ~48 hours of total coursework, the largest course on QCode. Like every QCode course, the JavaScript course is designed to be taken at a students own pace, so the total time can vary widely. There are a total of 3 sections to the course, beginner, intermediate, and advanced. The beginner section includes concepts such as variables, drawing, 2D coordinates, and functions. Intermediate focuses more on objects, arrays, and loops with larger and more advanced projects. The advanced section teaches object oriented programming, classes, and inheritance.

## Other languages
QCode offers more than just JavaScript. In total there are 5 courses:
- JavaScript
- Scratch
- Python
- Arduino
- Web (HTML,CSS,JS)

Students choose their course based on their own interests and their skill level. For example, students having completed the beginner section of the JavaScript course may transition to the Arduino course, learning engineering and robotics skills. Another example, Scratch, allows easy entry for younger students into the world of coding. The Scratch course is designed to work as a precursor to JavaScript, and students typically move to JavaScript when they are ready.

## Impact
Hundreds of students and parents have trusted QCode with their computer science education. Their trust has paid off, as those who stick with the program have shown significant improvement in the programming skills.

QCode is primarily used to teach paying customers at a [local small business](https://esporterz.com), but it has also been used at a non-profit to teach underrepresented teens programming. QCode has also taught many mentally disabled students, often with great success.
